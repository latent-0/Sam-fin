How it works

A preparation tool, not a lender and not licensed financial advice. It checks your figures against illustrative criteria and simulates the next twelve months.

Three questions, three methods

  1. How big an instalment is safe? Your income history is turned into thousands of possible next years. The safe instalment is the largest one for which cash runs out in only a small share of them. With fewer than 12 months of records we use a stricter margin, because short histories fool simple models.
  2. What would get me approved? A credit model trained on three public datasets scores the loan, then searches for the cheapest changes, such as a smaller instalment or a bigger cash buffer, that would move the score into the likely range.
  3. What should I do this month? A learned selector chooses which of six helpers (budgeting, debt, emergency fund, goal, income protection, investing) should act. Each helper's contribution is measured by simulating every combination.
  4. What if a bad stretch hits? The shock lab cuts income for the months you choose and reruns the simulation. A second model, the pattern reader, uses attention over your raw months to catch long lean spells and zero months.

Services behind the page

What to keep in mind

  • Income, spending and credit history are the only inputs. Nothing is looked up about you.
  • The household simulations and the loan labels use assumed behaviour. Treat percentages as guidance, not promises.
  • Income that alternates between long good and bad spells is the hardest case for the safe-instalment rule. If that is you, lean on the buffer advice.

The evidence

Results from the experiments in the accompanying paper. All are simulated or based on public datasets.
